Why this season

Open enrollment is when people switch plans and pick a new primary care doctor. Blue Shield is already sending you patients. We want to be the name they see while they're choosing.

02 · What they see

Three ads, all real photos of you and the office

Ad 1Meet your new doctor. "New patients welcome in Huntington Beach. Dr. Ameer Moussa, rated 5.0 on Google." Photo of you at the front desk with the team.
Ad 2Switching plans? "Picking a new plan for 2027? Make Coral Med your primary care doctor. We take Medicare and most major plans." Photo of the office.
Ad 3Same-day sick visits. "Sick and can't wait weeks? Call us for a same-day visit." Photo of you in your white coat.

Every ad has one button: Call now. No forms, so nobody types health information into Facebook.

What we won't do

No Facebook tracking code on your website, and no ads that hint we know someone's health condition. Both are privacy risks for a medical office, and Meta restricts them for healthcare anyway. Call tracking gives us cleaner numbers.

04 · What to expect

Facebook calls usually cost more than Google calls

Day 14 checkpoint

Keep itUnder $30 a call, new patients logged
Fix itCalls coming, few booking
Shut it offOver $50 a call, no new patients

Why the bar is higher

On Google, people are already searching for a doctor, so a call costs you about $10. On Facebook we're catching people who aren't searching yet. That's why the checkpoint is built in. If it can't beat the numbers above, the money goes back to Google.
